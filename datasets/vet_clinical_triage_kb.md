## 1. Acute Emergency & Toxic Ingestion Triage (Red Tier: Immediate Action)

### 1.1 Chocolate & Methylxanthine (Theobromine) Toxicity
- **Toxin**: Theobromine and caffeine found in cocoa solids.
- **Toxicity Thresholds (per kg body weight)**:
  - *Mild Signs (Agitation, vomiting, diarrhea, polydipsia)*: ~20 mg/kg
  - *Cardiovascular Distress (Tachycardia, arrhythmias, panting, hypertension)*: ~40-50 mg/kg
  - *Severe Neurotoxicity (Tremors, seizures, hyperthermia, death)*: >60 mg/kg
- **Chocolate Danger Spectrum**:
  - Cocoa Powder & Unsweetened Baking Chocolate: **HIGHEST RISK** (~14-28 mg theobromine per gram).
  - Dark Chocolate (70%+): **HIGH RISK** (~5.5 mg/gram). 50g can severely poison a 10kg dog.
  - Milk Chocolate: **MODERATE RISK** (~1.5-2.0 mg/gram).
  - White Chocolate: **LOW RISK** (Negligible theobromine, but high risk of acute pancreatitis due to fat content).
- **Immediate Action**:
  1. DO NOT wait for symptoms to appear.
  2. Transport immediately to an emergency facility for decontamination (induced emesis via apomorphine under veterinary supervision within 2-4 hours).
  3. Keep the animal cool and calm to minimize cardiovascular spike.

### 1.2 Lily (Lilium & Hemerocallis species) Toxicity in Felines
- **Affected Species**: EXCLUSIVELY FATAL TO CATS.
- **Mechanism**: Causes rapid, acute tubular necrosis and irreversible acute renal failure.
- **Toxic Parts**: Every single part (petals, leaves, stem, pollen, even water from the vase). Ingestion of less than 1 leaf or grooming pollen off fur is lethal.
- **Onset of Signs**: Vomiting within 1-3 hours, followed by lethargy, dehydration, anuria (no urine), and uremic crisis within 24-72 hours.
- **Immediate Action**: Extreme medical emergency. Rush to ICU for aggressive intravenous fluid diuresis within 18 hours of exposure.

### 1.3 Xylitol (Artificial Sweetener / Birch Sugar)
- **Common Sources**: Sugar-free chewing gum, peanut butter, low-calorie baked goods, human dental products.
- **Mechanism in Dogs**: Causes massive, rapid release of endogenous insulin resulting in profound hypoglycemia (collapse, seizures) within 30-60 minutes, followed by acute hepatic necrosis (liver failure).
- **Toxic Dose**: As low as 0.1 g/kg causes hypoglycemia; >0.5 g/kg causes acute liver failure.

### 1.4 Paracetamol (Acetaminophen / Panadol) in Cats & Dogs
- **Cats**: LACK the glucuronyl transferase enzyme needed to metabolize paracetamol. A single 500mg tablet causes fatal methemoglobinemia (chocolate-brown blood, cyanosis, facial edema, hypothermia, suffocation).
- **Antidote**: Intravenous N-acetylcysteine (NAC) must be administered immediately.

### 1.5 Rodenticides (Rat Poison)
- **Anticoagulant Rodenticides**: Inhibits Vitamin K epoxide reductase, depleting clotting factors II, VII, IX, X.
- **Signs**: Lethargy, pale gums, coughing blood, hematochezia, spontaneous subcutaneous bruising (petechiae) appearing 3-7 days post-ingestion.
- **Immediate Action**: Antidote is oral Vitamin K1 (Phytonadione) therapy for 3-4 weeks under coagulation monitoring.

## 2. Life-Threatening Non-Toxic Emergencies

### 2.1 Gastric Dilatation-Volvulus (GDV / Bloat)
- **High-Risk Breeds**: Large and giant deep-chested dogs (German Shepherds, Great Danes, Standard Poodles, Rottweilers).
- **Hallmark Triad**:
  1. Non-productive retching / dry heaving (trying to vomit white foam but unable to bring anything up).
  2. Distended, firm, tympanic (drum-like) cranial abdomen.
  3. Restlessness, pacing, panting, hunched posture, rapid weak pulse.
- **Mortality**: 100% fatal without surgical decompression and gastropexy within hours.

### 2.2 Feline Urethral Obstruction ("Blocked Cat")
- **High-Risk**: Young to middle-aged male castrated domestic cats.
- **Signs**: Repeatedly visiting the litter box, vocalizing, straining unproductive drops of urine (often confused by owners with constipation), licking penis, painful hard bladder.
- **Crisis**: Leads to life-threatening post-renal azotemia, hyperkalemia (cardiac arrest), and bladder rupture within 24-48 hours.

### 2.3 Respiratory Distress (Dyspnea)
- **Cat Specific**: Cats DO NOT normally breathe with their mouth open. Any open-mouth breathing or abdominal pumping in a cat is a Category 1 Red Alert for feline asthma, pleural effusion, or congestive heart failure.
- **Cyanosis**: Gums turning purple, blue, or slate grey indicates hypoxemia. Provide supplemental oxygen immediately.

### 2.4 Heatstroke (Hyperthermia)
- **Body Temperature**: Core rectal temp > 40.5°C (105°F).
- **High-Risk**: Brachycephalic breeds (Pugs, French Bulldogs, English Bulldogs, Persians) in hot/humid weather.
- **First-Aid Protocol**:
  - Move to air-conditioned room or shade.
  - Apply room-temperature tap water to paws, groin, and neck with gentle fan breeze.
  - **NEVER use ice or freezing water** (causes peripheral vasoconstriction which traps heat inside vital organs).
  - Stop cooling once rectal temp reaches 39.4°C (103°F) to avoid hypothermia rebound.

## 3. Moderate Clinical Care (Yellow Tier: 24-48 Hour Schedule)

- **Persistent Vomiting or Diarrhea**: >24 hours with normal hydration, no blood, and active demeanor.
- **Lameness / Limping**: Weight-bearing limp without open bone exposure or severe spinal pain.
- **Otitis Externa (Ear Infection)**: Head shaking, foul brown discharge, scratching ears.
- **Ocular Issues**: Squinting, conjunctival redness, clear to mucoid discharge (requires fluorescein stain to rule out corneal ulcer).
- **Dermatology**: Localized hot spots, flea allergy dermatitis, alopecia without systemic illness.

---

## 4. Low Urgency Home Monitoring & Wellness (Green Tier)

- Single episode of vomiting after rapid eating (food bolus regurgitation).
- Transient soft stool with normal activity and water consumption.
- Hairball coughing in long-haired cats (managed with petroleum-based malt paste or fiber).
- Minor superficial skin scratches without bleeding.
- Puppy/kitten routine vaccination scheduling and transition from mother's milk to starter mousse.

## 5. Approved First Aid Rules & Absolute Contraindications

1. **NEVER use Table Salt to Induce Vomiting**: Causes acute hypernatremia, brain dehydration, neurological collapse, and death.
2. **NEVER use 3% Hydrogen Peroxide in Cats**: Induces severe hemorrhagic necrotizing gastritis and esophagitis.
3. **NEVER give Human Painkillers**: Paracetamol, Ibuprofen (Advil), Naproxen (Aleve), and Aspirin cause acute renal necrosis, gastric perforation, and fatal methemoglobinemia.
